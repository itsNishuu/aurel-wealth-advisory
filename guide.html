What is Aurel?

Aurel is a planning tool a wealth adviser would use with a family. It answers one question honestly: if this family keeps investing the way it plans to, how likely is it to reach its goal, and what should change if the odds are poor?

01

The problem it solves

Most investment projections say something like “invest ₹30,000 a month at 12% a year and you will have ₹1 crore”. Markets do not move in a straight line. Some years are great, some are terrible, and the order in which they come matters. A single number hides all of that, so every plan looks on track.

Aurel instead runs 1,000 possible futures, like replaying the next twelve years a thousand times with different market luck, and shows the spread of results. That lets an adviser say something honest, such as “in 8 out of 10 futures you reach the goal”, and then work out what would raise those odds.

02

The story inside the app

The Rao household (fictional) has ₹15 lakh invested, saves ₹30,000 a month (rising 5% each year) and wants ₹1 crore in twelve years. They describe their appetite for risk as medium. Every number in the app can be changed in the Client brief panel on the left.

    PlanWill the family reach its goal?

The darker band holds the middle half of all futures, the lighter band holds 8 in 10, the line is the typical (median) path and the gold line is the goal. Below it: how much the family must save each month to reach the goal with 70%, 80% or 90% confidence, how big the gap is when they miss, and what waiting a few years to start would cost.

    How the savings are split across four types of investment. The key insight: where the money sits is not where the risk sits. Shares can be under half of the money yet cause almost all of the ups and downs.

    Hand-built shock scenarios, such as a market crash or a jump in interest rates, applied to today's portfolio. There is also a checker that compares any portfolio's monthly returns with a benchmark.

06

Key words, in everyday language

Monte Carlo simulation
Running a calculation many times with random but realistic inputs to see the range of possible results, not just one.
Percentile
A ranking point. The 10th percentile is a poor outcome (only 1 in 10 futures do worse); the 90th is a strong one.
Median
The middle result: half of the futures end higher, half lower.
Confidence level
How sure you want to be. Saving for 90% confidence means reaching the goal in 9 out of 10 simulated futures.
Today's rupees (real terms)
Future amounts adjusted for inflation, so they can be compared with what money buys now.
Asset allocation
How money is divided between types of investment, such as shares, bonds, gold and cash.
Volatility
How much an investment's value swings up and down. Higher volatility means a bumpier ride.
Risk contribution
How much of the portfolio's total swings come from each investment.
Rebalancing
Buying and selling to bring the mix back to the plan after markets have moved it.
Drawdown
The fall from a previous high point to a low, for example −20%.
Sharpe ratio
Return earned per unit of risk taken. Higher is better.
Benchmark
A standard to compare against, such as a market index.

07

Questions people ask

Is any of this real client data?

No. The Rao household is fictional and every market assumption is illustrative. Nothing here is investment advice.

Can I change the numbers?

Yes. Every input in the Client brief panel can be edited, and every chart, figure and the client letter recalculate immediately.

Why does the same input always give the same answer?

The simulation uses a seeded random number generator, so results are reproducible. That is also what makes the contribution solver reliable.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. The layout adapts to small screens, and the client letter can be printed or saved as a PDF from any device.

How do I know the maths is right?

The calculations live in one file of pure functions with an automated test suite that runs before every deployment. If a test fails, the site does not publish.

Who is it for?

Anyone curious about how wealth planning works, and recruiters or hiring managers looking at wealth management, investment analyst and portfolio analytics work.

08

What this project shows, and what it does not

What it shows

Turning planning ideas into a model that is numerical, explainable and tested, then explaining the result to someone who is not a specialist. Relevant to wealth management, investment analyst and portfolio analytics roles.

What it is not

It is not investment advice and not real client work. The family is fictional, the market assumptions are illustrative, and there is no historical backtest or tax treatment.
